Optimal parallelization strategies for active flow control in deep reinforcement learning-based computational fluid dynamics

Jia, W., & Xu, H. (2024). Physics of Fluids, 36(4), 043623 · Featured Article.

DOI: 10.1063/5.0204237

Publisher version (DOI)Open version (arXiv:2402.11515)PDFAll BibTeX

Abstract

Deep reinforcement learning (DRL) has emerged as a promising approach for handling highly dynamic and nonlinear active flow control (AFC) problems. However, the computational cost associated with training DRL models presents a significant performance bottleneck. To address this challenge and enable efficient scaling on high-performance computing architectures, this study focuses on optimizing DRL-based algorithms in parallel settings. We validate an existing state-of-the-art DRL framework used for AFC problems and discuss its efficiency bottlenecks. Subsequently, by deconstructing the overall framework and conducting extensive scalability benchmarks for individual components, we investigate various hybrid parallelization configurations and propose efficient parallelization strategies. Moreover, we refine input/output (I/O) operations in multi-environment DRL training to tackle critical overhead associated with data movement. Finally, we demonstrate the optimized framework for a typical AFC problem where near-linear scaling can be obtained for the overall framework. We achieve a significant boost in parallel efficiency from around 49% to approximately 78%, and the training process is accelerated by approximately 47 times using 60 central processing unit (CPU) cores. These findings are expected to provide valuable insight for further advancements in DRL-based AFC studies.

Key findings

  • The computational cost of training is the bottleneck of DRL-based active flow control. The paper validates an existing state-of-the-art framework and identifies where its efficiency is lost.
  • Scalability benchmarks of the individual components lead to efficient hybrid parallelization strategies, and input/output operations in multi-environment training are refined to cut the overhead of data movement.
  • Parallel efficiency rises from about 49% to about 78%, and training is accelerated about 47 times on 60 CPU cores, with near-linear scaling of the overall framework.
